Website vs. Just Using Instagram: Why Your Business Still Needs Both

September 2026 · Paul Danowski

Instagram is genuinely good at getting attention. It was never built to hold onto it, prove you're legitimate, or be found by someone who's never heard of you. That's a website's job, and no amount of posting replaces it.

A lot of small businesses quietly decide the website isn't worth it — the Instagram page has followers, it's free, and it's already up to date. That's a reasonable instinct and an expensive mistake, because a social feed and a website aren't two versions of the same thing. They do different jobs, and skipping one leaves real business on the table.

01You don't own that audience — Instagram does

Every follower, every post, every "link in bio" click lives on infrastructure you don't control. Algorithm changes, feature changes, a flagged account, an outage — any of it can cut off access to an audience you spent years building, overnight, with no appeal process that moves at the speed your business needs. A website is the one piece of your presence that's actually yours: your domain, your hosting, your rules.

02Google can't send anyone to your Instagram

Someone searching "[your business] hours" or "best [what you do] near me" is being served by Google, not Instagram — and Instagram profiles are built to keep people inside the app, not to rank in search results. Without a website, you're invisible to the entire category of customer who's looking for you but doesn't already follow you.

03Your hours, menu, and address are buried behind a login and a scroll

On a website, that information is one tap away, always in the same place. On Instagram, it's a pinned story, a link in the bio, or a caption from three weeks ago — assuming the visitor is even logged in and not hitting a "sign up to continue" wall first. Every extra tap between a hungry, curious, or just-arrived customer and the answer they need is a chance for them to give up and try someone else.

04It's built for browsing, not booking

A feed is designed to keep someone scrolling, not to get them to a decision. There's no menu order button, no booking calendar, no contact form — just more content. A website can put the one thing you actually want a visitor to do directly in front of them, above the fold, every time.

05Some customers still won't trust a business without one

Fair or not, a business with only a social page — no domain, no real site — reads to a meaningful slice of customers as unfinished, or not fully real. That's especially true for anyone comparing you to a competitor who does have a site, or a customer used to Googling a business before they call. A website is still doing quiet work as a credibility check, even for people who found you on Instagram first.

They're not competing for the same job

Instagram is where people discover you, watch you work, and decide they like you. A website is where that interest turns into a phone call, a booking, or a walk in the door — and where you show up for the customer who was never on Instagram at all. Running one without the other isn't a shortcut. It's half a presence.
